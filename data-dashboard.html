🔍 Key Findings

1. Strong Negative Correlation: Leadership ↔ Burnout (r = -0.66)

Higher leadership effectiveness scores are associated with lower employee burnout and dissatisfaction. This suggests that leadership quality is a critical factor in employee wellbeing in healthcare settings.

2. COVID-19 Sustained Impact on Turnover (+0.69pp)

Healthcare worker quit rates increased by 0.69 percentage points from pre-COVID levels and remain elevated. This represents the "Great Resignation" phenomenon specifically affecting healthcare workers.

3. Burnout Surge Post-Pandemic (+0.98 points)

Employee burnout and dissatisfaction scores increased by nearly 1 full point (on a 5-point scale) since 2019. This dramatic increase suggests systemic stress within healthcare organizations.

4. Moderate Positive Correlation: Burnout ↔ Quit Rate (r = 0.53)

Higher burnout levels correlate with higher quit rates, validating the use of turnover data as a proxy measure for employee burnout in healthcare settings.

5. Leadership Decline During Crisis (-0.15 points)

Leadership effectiveness scores decreased during the pandemic period, potentially due to increased organizational stress, resource constraints, and competing demands on healthcare leaders.

💡 Implications for Evidence-Based Management

Investment in Leadership Development

The strong negative correlation between leadership effectiveness and burnout suggests that investing in leadership training and development programs could significantly reduce employee burnout and turnover in healthcare organizations.

Targeted Interventions During Crisis Periods

The data shows leadership effectiveness declined during COVID-19 while burnout spiked. Organizations should implement enhanced leadership support systems during crisis periods to maintain effectiveness.

Monitoring and Early Warning Systems

Organizations should track both objective metrics (quit rates) and subjective measures (leadership ratings, burnout scores) to identify problems early and implement timely interventions.
