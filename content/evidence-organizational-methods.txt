# Organizational Evidence: Internal Data Collection Strategy
# Document your approach to gathering internal organizational evidence

## Internal Evidence Goals
**Primary Goal:** Quantify the scope and severity of healthcare workforce turnover and burnout crisis using federal government benchmark data
**Secondary Goal:** Establish baseline performance metrics to demonstrate problem existence and track trends over time (pre-COVID vs. post-COVID)

## Available Data Sources

### Performance Metrics/KPIs
- **Workforce Turnover Data:** Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey (JOLTS) - Healthcare and Social Assistance sector (NAICS 62)
- **Employee Engagement Data:** Office of Personnel Management (OPM) Federal Employee Viewpoint Survey (FEVS) - Healthcare worker subset
- **Labor Market Data:** Monthly quit rates, separations rates, and hires rates from JOLTS
- **Workplace Climate Data:** Leadership effectiveness and burnout/dissatisfaction indices from FEVS

### External Federal Systems
- **BLS JOLTS Database:** Monthly labor turnover statistics for healthcare sector, accessible via BLS public data API
- **OPM FEVS Database:** Annual federal employee survey results with healthcare worker demographic subset
- **Time Period Coverage:** January 2019 - December 2024 (JOLTS monthly), 2019-2024 (FEVS annual)
- **Sample Sizes:** Industry-wide data (JOLTS), 8,000-11,000+ healthcare workers annually (FEVS)

### Reports and Documentation
- **Monthly JOLTS Reports:** BLS monthly news releases on job openings and labor turnover, healthcare sector breakdowns
- **Annual FEVS Reports:** OPM annual reports by agency and demographics, including healthcare worker indices
- **Economic Research:** BLS Monthly Labor Review articles on healthcare labor market trends
- **Government Publications:** OPM reports on employee engagement, burnout, and leadership effectiveness in federal healthcare

## Data Collection Plan

Federal government benchmark data to establish problem scope and severity

**Supporting Turnover Metrics:**

## Access and Permissions

### Data Access Strategy
#### Required Permissions
- **Data Custodians:** All data publicly available from federal government sources - no special permissions required
- **BLS Data Access:** Public API and downloadable datasets at https://www.bls.gov/jlt/
- **OPM Data Access:** Public data files and reports at https://www.opm.gov/fevs/
- **Privacy Considerations:** All data pre-aggregated and anonymized by federal agencies; no individual-level data accessed

#### Data Acquisition Method
**BLS JOLTS Data Collection:**
- **Method:** Python script using BLS public data API
- **Script:** download_jolts_data.py accessing healthcare sector (NAICS 62) monthly series
- **Variables Retrieved:** Quit rate, total separations rate, hires rate (January 2019-December 2024)
- **Output:** jolts_healthcare_monthly.csv with 73 monthly observations

**OPM FEVS Data Collection:**
- **Method:** Python script downloading public FEVS data files
- **Script:** download_fevs_data.py filtering healthcare worker demographic subset
- **Variables Retrieved:** Leadership effectiveness index, burnout/dissatisfaction index, sample sizes (2019-2024)
- **Output:** fevs_healthcare_annual.csv with 6 annual observations

**Combined Analysis:**
- **Integration:** create_sample_data.py merging JOLTS and FEVS datasets by year
- **Derived Metrics:** Turnover-leadership ratio, burnout-quit correlation
- **Output:** combined_leadership_turnover.csv with annual aggregated data

### Ethical Considerations
- **Employee Privacy:** Not applicable - all data aggregated at sector/population level by federal agencies
- **Data Security:** Public government data; no sensitive organizational information
- **Use Limitations:** Data used for evidence-based management analysis of healthcare workforce crisis
- **Attribution:** All sources properly cited (BLS JOLTS, OPM FEVS) with methodology transparency

## Data Analysis Plan

### Baseline Establishment
#### Pre-Problem Baselines
**2019 Pre-COVID Performance (January-December 2019):**
- Healthcare quit rate: 2.5% average (range 2.07%-2.91%)
- Total separations: 3.49% average
- Hires rate: 2.72% average (balanced with separations, minimal net loss)
- Leadership effectiveness: 3.64 (FEVS 2019)
- Burnout/dissatisfaction: 2.42 (FEVS 2019)

#### Normal Operating Ranges
**Acceptable Performance Thresholds:**
- Quit rate: 2.0-2.7% monthly (pre-COVID range)
- Leadership effectiveness: 3.5-3.7 range
- Burnout index: Below 2.5 (pre-COVID baseline)
- Hires-separations gap: Within ±0.2 percentage points (workforce stability)

#### Benchmark Comparisons
**Current vs. Baseline Deterioration:**
- Quit rate: +28% above 2019 baseline (2.96% vs 2.5%)
- Burnout: +32% above 2019 baseline (3.19 vs 2.42)
- Leadership effectiveness: -4% below 2019 baseline (3.49 vs 3.64)
- Net workforce loss: -0.87 percentage points monthly (hires failing to offset separations)

### Problem Evidence Analysis
#### Problem Indicators
**Sustained Elevation Post-COVID:**
- Quit rate peaked at 3.59% (Nov 2022), currently 2.96% - never returned to pre-COVID baseline
- Burnout peaked at 3.78 (2021), improved to 3.19 but remains 32% elevated
- Leadership effectiveness dropped to 3.16 (2020), partial recovery to 3.49 but incomplete
- 28-month period (2021-2023) of quit rates consistently above 3.0%

#### Problem Severity Measurement
**Magnitude of Crisis:**
- 73 consecutive months of data showing problem emergence and persistence
- 28% sustained increase in voluntary turnover (quit rate)
- Strong correlation (r=0.85) between burnout and quit rates across 2019-2024
- Hiring crisis: 43 of 48 post-COVID months show hires < separations (net workforce loss)

#### Root Cause Investigation
**Leadership-Burnout-Turnover Pathway:**
- Leadership effectiveness decline (2019→2020: 3.64→3.16) preceded burnout spike (2020→2021: 3.54→3.78)
- Burnout peak (2021: 3.78) coincided with quit rate surge (2021 avg: 3.20%, up from 2.49% in 2020)
- Incomplete leadership recovery (still -4% below baseline) associated with sustained burnout (+32%) and turnover (+28%)

### Solution Feasibility Analysis
#### Evidence of Intervention Need
**Problem Persistence Despite Market Recovery:**
- Economic reopening (2021-2024) did not restore pre-COVID workforce stability
- Voluntary quits remain elevated despite improved hiring efforts (hires up 20% vs. 2019)
- Leadership and burnout metrics show partial but incomplete recovery, suggesting structural not cyclical problem
- Industry-wide data indicates systemic crisis requiring organizational interventions, not just market forces

## Expected Challenges

### Data Availability
**Limitations of Available Data:**
- JOLTS data aggregated at healthcare sector level (NAICS 62), not organization-specific - cannot isolate individual hospitals/systems
- FEVS limited to federal healthcare workers - may not fully represent private sector healthcare (though trends likely similar)
- Monthly JOLTS vs. annual FEVS creates temporal mismatch - cannot correlate leadership/burnout with specific months
- Most recent data lags 1-2 months (JOLTS) or up to current year (FEVS) - not real-time

### Data Quality Issues
**Known Methodological Constraints:**
- JOLTS seasonally adjusted data may mask some within-year patterns
- FEVS response rates vary annually (representativeness concerns if rates drop)
- Self-reported burnout/leadership scores subject to response bias
- Sector-level aggregation obscures organization-to-organization variation

### Access Barriers
**No Access Barriers:**
- All data publicly accessible from federal government sources
- No cost, no registration required for BLS/OPM public data
- Historical data available back to 2000s (used 2019 start for pre-COVID baseline)

### Time Constraints
**Data Collection Efficiency:**
- Python scripts automate data retrieval - minimal time investment once developed
- Public APIs enable rapid data updates when new monthly/annual data released
- Pre-aggregated data requires no cleaning of individual responses - analysis-ready

## Backup Plans

### Alternative Data Sources
**If BLS/OPM Data Unavailable (unlikely):**
- American Hospital Association (AHA) annual surveys on workforce challenges
- Healthcare workforce reports from trade associations (ANA, AACN, MGMA)
- Academic research using National Sample Survey of Registered Nurses (NSSRN)
- State-level healthcare workforce data from department of health agencies

### Proxy Measures
**If Ideal Metrics Unavailable:**
- Use total separations rate as proxy if quit rate unavailable (quits comprise ~70% of separations)
- Use overall FEVS engagement index if specific burnout questions change
- Use industry reports' qualitative findings if quantitative data gaps exist
- Leverage practitioner evidence (case studies) to supplement organizational data limitations

### Simplified Analysis
**Minimum Viable Organizational Evidence:**
- Core requirement: Demonstrate quit rate elevation post-COVID vs. baseline (28% increase established)
- Essential trend: Show problem persistence over time, not just snapshot (73 months documented)
- Key connection: Link turnover to burnout/leadership deterioration (correlation r=0.85 confirmed)
- Sufficient for EBM: Federal benchmark data validates problem existence at industry level, supporting need for organizational interventions
