STAKEHOLDER MAPPING FOR HEALTHCARE WORKFORCE BURNOUT/TURNOVER CRISIS
================================================================================

STAKEHOLDER MAP (Concentric Circles)
----------------------------------------

INTERNAL STAKEHOLDERS (Direct Impact, Primary Responsibility):
• Healthcare Employees (nurses, physicians, clinical staff)
  - Directly experiencing burnout and making turnover decisions
  - Primary responsibility: Organization's core workforce

• Leadership/Management
  - Directly impacted by retention costs and implementation responsibility
  - Primary responsibility: Decision-makers and intervention implementers

CONNECTED STAKEHOLDERS (Direct/Indirect Impact, Primary/Secondary Responsibility):
• Patients
  - Directly impacted by care quality and continuity
  - Primary responsibility: Service recipients

• Staffing Agencies
  - Direct impact through temporary placement demand
  - Secondary responsibility: Workforce suppliers

• Academic Institutions
  - Indirect impact through training disruption
  - Secondary responsibility: Education partners

EXTERNAL STAKEHOLDERS (Indirect Impact, Secondary Responsibility):
• Professional Associations (AACN, ANA, AMA)
  - Indirect impact, advocacy role
  - Secondary responsibility: Professional representation

• Regulatory Agencies (JCAHO, CMS)
  - Indirect impact through quality standards
  - Secondary responsibility: Oversight bodies

• Insurance Payers
  - Indirect impact through quality and cost
  - Secondary responsibility: Reimbursement entities

• Technology Vendors
  - Indirect impact (EHR administrative burden)
  - Secondary responsibility: System suppliers

• Local Community
  - Indirect impact through healthcare access
  - Secondary responsibility: Society at large


================================================================================

POWER/INTEREST DIAGRAM QUADRANTS
----------------------------------------

MANAGE CLOSELY (High Power, High Interest):
• Healthcare Leadership/Senior Executives/Hospital Administrators
  - Control resources and budgets for interventions
  - Directly impacted by turnover costs ($52,350 per RN)
  - Accountable for workforce crisis resolution
  - Strategy: Engage deeply in decision-making, regular updates

KEEP SATISFIED (High Power, Low Interest):
• Regulatory Agencies (JCAHO, CMS)
  - High power through accreditation and reimbursement
  - Lower interest (not focused on this specific issue)
• Insurance Payers
  - Financial leverage but may prioritize other quality metrics
  - Strategy: Keep satisfied, meet their requirements

KEEP INFORMED (Low Power, High Interest):
• Healthcare Employees (Nurses, Physicians, Clinical Staff)
  - Directly experiencing burnout (32% above baseline)
  - Limited organizational decision authority
  - High interest in solutions that reduce workload
• Patients
  - Affected by care quality but limited organizational influence
  - Strategy: Transparent communication, gather feedback

MONITOR (Low Power, Low Interest):
• Technology Vendors
  - Peripheral involvement unless workflow changes required
• Staffing Agencies
  - Benefit from status quo (turnover creates demand)
• Local Community
  - Broad concern but not focused on specific issue
• Academic Institutions
  - Affected by clinical placement disruption but limited power
  - Strategy: Monitor, minimal effort

MEDIUM POSITIONS:
• Professional Associations (AACN, ANA, AMA)
  - Moderate power through advocacy
  - High interest in workforce well-being
• Mid-Level Managers
  - Moderate power (implement but don't set strategy)
  - High interest (responsible for daily operations)
